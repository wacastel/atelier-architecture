# Adler Planetarium

The Museum Campus location includes an architectural reconstruction of Adler's historic core, the lake-side Sky Pavilion and the exterior of Doane Observatory. The upper entrance, welcome gallery, instrument exhibits and central domed theater are navigable. The interior arrangement is an interpretation guided by Adler's public visitor map; it is not an as-built survey or an emergency-access plan.

## Reference basis

- The [National Park Service nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/87000819_text) describes the 160 ft dodecagon, three receding rings, reddish rainbow granite, corner reliefs, fluting, copper hemisphere and broad western stairs. The model uses these characteristic forms rather than a generic solid extrusion.
- [Wight, the renovation architect](https://www.wightco.com/work/adler-planetarium/), documents the 21 m replacement projection dome, replacement of the exterior copper roof, and the low glass C-shaped Sky Pavilion. Its actual aerial and west-entrance photographs were viewed, including the warm copper roof, ribbed inclined glazing and tiered stone facade. The reference page's projected theater and colored welcome-gallery photographs were also viewed.
- [Cosm, the projection supplier](https://tech.cosm.com/our-work/adler-planetarium), specifies the 21 m, 191-degree dome and an audience of nearly 200. Its image links failed to load, so no visual observation is attributed to those images.
- A [firsthand DePaul evening visit photograph](https://blogs.depaul.edu/deblogs/project/visiting-the-adler-planetarium/) was also actually viewed. Its upper copper dome is dark while white practical light reveals the doors and stone stairs. Two modest modeled entry downlights follow this balance; their exact placement and intensity are interpretations.
- Adler's current [upper-level visitor map](https://www.adlerplanetarium.org/plan-your-visit/museum-map/) was actually viewed. It places the domed theater in the original core, with the welcome gallery and surrounding exhibits connected to the crescent. The exact historical exhibits, commercial show contents and decorative installations are not reproduced.
- The bundled September 8, 2026 OpenStreetMap derivative supplies the historic core (way 766703208), dome (way 686342956), combined building/pavilion (way 24825591) and Doane Observatory (way 91526195). The projection center follows the mapped dome at east 2413.718 m, south 1393.247 m relative to the shared Chicago origin. A mapped absolute elevation is not treated as a local floor height.

## Geometry and circulation

The 48.768 m historic diameter is represented by twelve stone faces, receding terrace rings, cornices, corner panels with original abstract constellation reliefs, and a smooth 26.6 m copper outer dome with standing seams. Its top is about 28.7 m above scene street grade, including the interpreted raised entrance terrace. The outer roof and the 21 m inner projection shell are separate surfaces. An opaque backing prevents the projection from appearing on the screen exterior seen from the welcome gallery. The inner shell extends 5.5 degrees below each side of a hemisphere, with an interpreted doorway opening at its west/north rim.

The representative upper floor is 2.8 m above street grade. Open bronze-framed entrance leaves, gallery portals and the theater doorway connect to clear aisles. The gallery contains original geometric orbit models, telescopes, plinths and consoles. Theater seats face forward in blocks around a central aisle; exact seat locations are not a claim about current operational seating. The crescent has sloped transparent panes, radial ribs, subdivided glazing, a floor and lower stone fascia. Doane is an exterior study only.

The granite's vein pattern, copper aging, exhibit models, abstract reliefs, door arrangement, artificial-light intensities and detailed interior finishes are illustrative. Grounded dimensions and mapped envelopes are separated from these artistic choices. Existing imported maps retain their OpenStreetMap attribution.

## Original dome show and renderer

The show is an original, silent, 180-second procedural composition: a rotating starfield, a broad galactic band, auroral curtains, abstract line constellations and a stylized ringed planet. It contains no extracted Adler show frames, branded show story, narration or licensed music. The planet motion is artistic, not an ephemeris or an astronomy lesson. At time zero the starfield is already visible. The dedicated theater walkthrough presents the complete 180-second program and returns to its opening camera pose.

`setSceneTime(_:)` drives both the dome and existing traffic from absolute seconds. The renderer reduces the show phase in Double before converting to Float; negative times and large finite times remain bounded. Same-time calls leave accumulation intact. Changing time invalidates progressive accumulation even with a stationary camera. Rewind and offline export use the same deterministic function. The new final `FrameUniforms.animation` field is a 16-byte aligned SIMD4 at offset 128; the complete uniform is 144 bytes. Existing traffic, sampling, lighting and material flags retain their prior fields and meanings.

Projection radiance is evaluated on the actual inward-facing dome geometry and participates in secondary ray transport. The screen is a terminal radiance display: its projected image is not relit by other lamps, while secondary paths can carry its light onto surrounding surfaces. Stars use equal-area spherical cells, a range of apparent magnitudes, and footprint-filtered discs to preserve stable subpixel coverage. Stable 3D chord distances and spherical-cap candidate bounds avoid the bright slivers that a longitude/latitude tangent approximation produced near the pole. The GPU regression samples a circular angular ring around a polar star and includes the defective approximation as a self-contained negative control. Always-on interior fixtures keep exhibits and theater aisle silhouettes legible in both daylight and night mode.

While the show advances, the screen, local emitters, sharp reflections and transmission retain exact current coverage. Nearby stationary, rough, nonemissive dielectrics reject temporal lighting history but allow the existing spatial filter to reduce current-frame noise on seats and floors. Its material, albedo, normal and depth boundaries remain active. Unrelated city surfaces retain their history, and paused projection and reflection guides resume their normal convergence behavior. The reflected-ray check first intersects a conservative 10.7 m sphere around the projection dome and requires a positive forward interval before traversing the scene, avoiding extra reflection queries for unrelated city windows. This first reflected-ray guide does not represent every possible rough or multi-bounce visibility path.

## Validation

The isolated assembly contains 105,796 triangles and 63 light fixtures, including 33 always-on interior lights.

Run `./scripts/validate-adler.sh` for geometry, mapped-neighborhood bounds, unit normals, the inward 21 m shell and connected entrance/gallery/theater route checks. `--gpu` also checks actual deterministic show advancement, exact rewind/loop images, paused accumulation, bounded timeline inputs and local/reflected reactive guides. Actual temporal/spatial GPU passes must reject stale diffuse lighting, reduce its image and temporal error, preserve material edges, and leave projection/reflection coverage and all previous guide classes unchanged; self-contained negative controls reproduce the missing temporal guard and the original complete filter bypass. `--baseline path/to/previous/Renderer.metal` compares controlled day/night hit/miss output with the pre-Adler shader using identical geometry, acceleration structure and sample seeds. `--render output/folder` creates isolated exterior and interior proofs for visual inspection. These fixture images omit the surrounding city; final full-world route and visual checks remain separate.
