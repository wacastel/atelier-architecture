# Lincoln Park Zoo, Nature Boardwalk and historic gardens

The North Side scene adds a connected architectural setting around Lincoln Park Zoo: Café Brauer, the South Pond wood boardwalk and Fisher Bridge, Studio Gang's education pavilion, the historic Lion House and Primate House, the bird house and other mapped zoo buildings, the Conservatory's glass houses, and the Alfred Caldwell Lily Pool. This is an architectural interpretation at mapped metre scale, with restrained representative animals and an original seasonal night installation.

Building heights, roof subdivisions, material color, doors/window modules, exhibit landscaping, animal poses, furniture, paths immediately around the small pavilions, and lamp placement are photographic or design interpretations. They are not construction drawings or a current operational zoo inventory. The accessible experience follows public outdoor paths; the zoo houses and Conservatory are architectural exteriors with visible planted glazing, not surveyed navigable interiors. Two lion figures, two giraffe figures, and pond waterfowl are original procedural approximations; their placement is not a claim about a real animal's current location. The model does not reproduce logos or sponsor artwork.

South Pond is authored at −0.55 m and the Lily Pool at −0.25 m relative to street grade, with the wood boardwalk at +0.12 m. These approximate relative levels follow the photographed shallow shores and avoid incorrectly applying Lake Michigan's −5.7 m level to ornamental ponds. The separate Fisher Bridge deck is +3.2 m, with an open underpass above the lower boardwalk. Neither elevation is a surveyed engineering datum. The source-derived planting exclusion network covers all mapped public footways, the actual vehicle-road centerlines and widths from the Chicago/Lakefront/NorthSide context resources, and ancillary building hulls, including circulation outside the tour. Layered mature deciduous crowns, branching trunks, native shrubs, flowers and grasses fill the authored site while keeping those buffers open. Individual tree placement and species-like forms are original landscape interpretations, not a surveyed tree inventory.

General zoo footways and surrounding terrain/water remain the responsibility of `NorthSideEnvironment.swift`.

## Night interpretation

Broad warm/neutral-white sources inside the Conservatory illuminate actual planted beds and iron/glass framing; finite-range exterior washes and garden fixtures reveal the roof silhouette, flower beds and fountain without making the glass or foliage emissive. Warm-white practical fixtures illuminate the pavilion, Café Brauer, building entries and selected public paths. An **original seasonal display inspired by ZooLights** adds seven colored arches and fourteen modest illuminated ornamental trees on the zoo mall. It is neither a year-round lighting claim nor a replica of a current festival layout. The real zoo's 2025 event is the visual reference; the chosen RGB arrangement, fixture positions and photometry are authored. The Lily Pool retains low, restrained warm path/gate light and is not covered by the zoo's colored installation.

All source lamps use finite ranges and the existing light grid; lens material pattern 16 preserves the chosen colors and emits only at night. Historic masonry uses the new footprint-filtered brick pattern 19. This component changes no renderer, clock, traffic, or temporal-reconstruction code. Animals and event strings are static geometry, so pause/rewind behavior inherits the existing deterministic scene timeline without introducing another clock.

## Validation

Run `./scripts/validate-lincoln-park-zoo.sh`. Regenerate the checked-in map helper with `python3 scripts/prepare-lincoln-park-zoo-map.py`. The isolated CPU fixture compiles the actual builder and the new component sources, checks finite unit-normal vertices and valid materials, verifies all eleven mapped landmark IDs and polygon indices, bounds geometry/light cost, and probes the four proposed routes at 0.35 m intervals using the production head/torso/leg collision rays in four horizontal directions. The actual foliage probe mesh must be watertight (each undirected edge used twice), have noncollapsed triangles, and share exact poles for all longitudes. A negative control retains the rejected angular-radius formula and confirms its separated pole heights. Vehicle-road probes reject both trees and shrubs inside the mapped carriageways. Both Fisher approach ramps additionally require a horizontal 8.8 m deck width, the intended grade envelope, and downward support across their width; a negative control reproduces the rejected sideways beam reference that had turned them into walls. It checks component geometry; final integrated route tests additionally cover terrain, general footways, trees and neighboring structures supplied by the full world.

The current component contains 1,214,333 triangles and 127 finite-range night lights. Rendering appearance and motion quality require the parent task's full-world day/night galleries and actual GPU validation. No visual quality or frame-rate claim is inferred from CPU checks.

The [final zoo motion visual review](validation/v1.7/zoo-motion-visual-review.json) records nine actually viewed raw/reconstructed/reference PNGs from the pavilion, ZooLights and Conservatory tests, with exact hashes and links to the numerical sequence results. Noise is visibly reduced, while some narrow shadows and masonry/timber detail remain softer than the high-sample reference. This is a review of one saved frame per test, not every frame of an animation.
